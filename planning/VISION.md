# Vision — Scope Creep

**Pitch:** A composition is being overrun. You are a blue circle on a sheet of graph paper, and the shapes of a Bauhaus poster have turned hostile — red wedges, black darts, heavy yellow blocks. Shoot them off the page, collect the shards they leave, bolt on upgrades with names like *Rhythm*, *Weight* and *Repetition*, and see how long you last before you are erased.

**Genre:** Twin-stick roguelite, short runs (10–20 minutes), endless escalation.

**Platforms:** Desktop and mobile browsers, equally. One thumb moves, the other aims and fires.

## Pillars

1. **Readable chaos.** Hundreds of shapes on screen, but you can always see yourself, the danger and the shards. Shape and color carry meaning: red = aggressive, black = fast, yellow = heavy, blue = you.
2. **Every level-up is a decision.** Upgrades stack into builds that feel different from run to run.
3. **Bauhaus, procedurally.** Flat primary colors and ink on warm paper, thick outlines, a hard paper-cut shadow. Everything is drawn in code. No glow, no gradients, no neon.
4. **Juice through form.** Hits flash black, shapes shatter into fragments, rings ripple out, the screen shakes when something heavy falls.
5. **Plays great on a phone.** Portrait or landscape, twin thumbs, 60 fps on a mid-range phone, large touch targets.
6. **Depth over count.** A new enemy brings a new behavior; a new upgrade changes what you do or decide. Ten enemies that each ask something different beat thirty that differ only in numbers, shape or color. Pairs of stat bonuses are not upgrades.
7. **Nothing unexplained.** Every key, button and HUD element is shown and taught where it is used, and listed on the pause screen. Abilities you trigger come from an upgrade you pick or a hero you choose before the run, never from nowhere.

## Tone

Art-school playful: names from design and art theory (*Point*, *Line*, *Composition*, *Balance*), clean geometric type, a dry sense of humor ("ERASED").
